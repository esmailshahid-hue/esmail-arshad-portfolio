## Challenge

A six-bundle lot of Brown Fantasy, worth roughly $18K and already committed to a commercial installation, arrived severely damaged. Replacing it meant another international shipment during COVID, when replenishment lead times were long and unreliable. The customer's installation date would not survive the wait.

## What I did

1. **Inspected the full lot with the warehouse team**, separating material that was genuinely unusable from sections with enough intact surface area to still serve.
2. **Recorded the usable dimensions** of the salvageable pieces rather than writing the shipment off as a single loss.
3. **Searched existing inventory** and found an older undamaged Brown Fantasy lot that could cover the main installation areas.
4. **Built an alternative fulfilment plan** pairing the older stock with the recovered sections from the damaged shipment.
5. **Worked with the warehouse, inventory and sales teams** to match available pieces against the customer's actual requirements and present the material's condition openly.

## Decision

> Rather than treat the shipment as either usable or a write-off, I matched surviving surface area against what the installation actually required.

## Outcome

The customer accepted the proposed mix of undamaged and recovered material and **completed the installation without waiting for another international shipment**.

About **one-third of the damaged lot was recovered as usable material**, reducing what would otherwise have been written off, and keeping a roughly **$50K project** on its original schedule.
