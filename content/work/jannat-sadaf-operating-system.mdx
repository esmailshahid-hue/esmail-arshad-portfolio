## Context

Jannat & Sadaf is a made-to-order apparel operation running three product lines with materially different economics. Pret is volume-led and price-sensitive. Formal and Wedding are lower volume, higher value, and carry the longest lead times in the business — a wedding order has a date attached to it that cannot move.

Over seven months the business grew revenue sixteenfold. The operating system underneath it was built during that period, not before it.

## Problem

Made-to-order work has no finished-goods buffer. Every order is a promise against capacity that does not exist yet, and the constraint moves — fabric availability one week, machine hours the next.

## What I did

1. **Rebuilt production scheduling** to sequence orders by due date, lead time and available capacity together, rather than in arrival order — with long-lead wedding wear placed first because it has the least schedule flexibility.
2. **Expanded the fabric supplier base from four vendors to twelve**, sourcing on cost for Pret and on quality for Formal and Wedding, so the two sourcing objectives stopped competing for the same supplier relationships.
3. **Implemented purchase-to-order cost tracking**, linking each material purchase to the specific order and SKU it was bought for — which is what makes product-level margin visible in a business where every order is bespoke.
4. **Held the operating rhythm through growth**, with scheduling, sourcing and cost capture running as one system rather than three separate improvements.

## Result

**98.6% on-time delivery.**

Alongside it, from the same operating system: fabric costs down 25%, rework held under 5% of order value, and spend traceability at 93.5% to order and SKU level.

The delivery figure is the one worth reading in context. Holding on-time delivery near 99% is unremarkable in a steady operation and materially harder while the order book multiplies — capacity assumptions expire, supplier lead times stretch, and the schedule has to be re-cut against constraints that changed since it was set. The growth is the context the number sits in. It is not a result claimed here.
