## Context

Amazon Freight prices and tenders middle-mile volume across a lane network handling roughly 100,000 shipments a week.

## Problem

Pricing rules needed to reflect lane economics and carrier behaviour rather than apply uniformly across the network. Lanes differ widely in length, density and how much time a driver loses at either end, and a rate that ignores those differences either fails to cover the load or overpays for it.

## What I did

- Analysed lane-level demand to establish where volume concentrated and where carrier capacity was thin.
- Brought mileage, lead time and dwell time into the pricing inputs, so each lane was priced against what it actually costs a carrier to run.
- Adjusted pricing rules dynamically against those inputs rather than holding them static between rate-card cycles.
- Measured at network level, which is the only level where a pricing rule's effect separates from shipment noise.

## Result

**8% reduction in freight network cost**, through better carrier capacity alignment.

## Related work

A separate workstream in the same role ran root-cause analysis across roughly 100,000 weekly shipments in Tableau and Excel, covering carrier performance, shipment and service exceptions and SLA adherence, and sustaining a 98% on-time delivery rate. It shares a network and a dataset with the pricing work but was not the cause of the pricing result.
