## Challenge

Jannat & Sadaf makes apparel to order across three lines. Pret is volume-led and price-sensitive; Formal and Wedding are lower volume, higher value, and carry the longest lead times, with dates that cannot move.

Made-to-order production has no finished-goods buffer, so a scheduling miss lands directly on a customer date. Sequencing had to weigh due date, lead time and available capacity at once — and I was building these routines while the business was growing, not before.

## What I did

1. **Rebuilt production scheduling** to sequence orders by due date, lead time and available capacity together, placing long-lead wedding wear first.
2. **Expanded the fabric supplier base from four vendors to twelve**, sourcing on cost for Pret and on quality for Formal and Wedding.
3. **Linked material purchases to specific orders and SKUs**, so cost could be tracked and margin read at product level.

## Decision

> Long-lead wedding orders hold the least flexibility of anything in the book, so they set the sequence and everything else filled in around them.

## Outcome

**98.6% on-time delivery**, held through a period in which revenue grew sixteenfold over seven months.

From the same changes: fabric costs down 25%, rework under 5% of order value, and spend traceable to order and SKU for 93.5% of purchases. The growth is the condition the delivery number was achieved under, not something I claim to have delivered.
