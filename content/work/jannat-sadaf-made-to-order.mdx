## Context

Jannat & Sadaf makes apparel to order across three lines. Pret is volume-led and price-sensitive; Formal and Wedding are lower volume, higher value, and carry the longest lead times, with dates that cannot move. I joined as the business was growing, and built the operating routines during that period rather than before it.

## Problem

Made-to-order production has no finished-goods buffer, so a scheduling miss lands directly on a customer date. Sequencing had to weigh due date, lead time and available capacity at the same time, with long-lead wedding orders holding the least flexibility of anything in the book.

## What I did

- Rebuilt production scheduling to sequence orders by due date, lead time and available capacity together, placing long-lead wedding wear first.
- Expanded the fabric supplier base from four vendors to twelve, sourcing on cost for Pret and on quality for Formal and Wedding.
- Linked material purchases to specific orders and SKUs, so cost could be tracked and margin read at product level.

## Result

**98.6% on-time delivery**, held through a period in which revenue grew sixteenfold over seven months.

Alongside it, from the same changes: fabric costs down 25%, rework under 5% of order value, and spend traceable to order and SKU for 93.5% of purchases. The growth is the condition the delivery number was achieved under, not something I claim to have delivered.
