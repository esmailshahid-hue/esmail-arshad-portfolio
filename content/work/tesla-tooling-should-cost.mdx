## Context

I worked on sourcing for top-level assembly tooling in new product introduction. The equipment is bespoke, the qualified supplier list is short, and the programme timeline makes walking away from a supplier expensive.

## Problem

Comparing bespoke tooling quotes against each other tells you which supplier is cheapest. It does not tell you whether the price is fair. With no comparable prior purchase to price against, there was no benchmark to assess a quote at all.

## What I did

- Broke the tooling down into machining, material and labour inputs instead of treating each quote as a single number.
- Benchmarked every input independently, so the resulting position did not depend on any supplier's own figures.
- Assessed the quotes against that benchmark, which surfaced charges the quoted totals did not show.
- Used the gaps as negotiation levers, taking the discussion line by line through the cost build-up.

## Result

**$150K in procurement savings.**

The model changed what the negotiation covered. Instead of asking a supplier to justify a discount, I could question a specific cost input against an independent estimate of it.

## Related work

Separate sourcing work in the same role covered supplier strategy and negotiation with contract manufacturers across Tier 1 and Tier 2 for NPI tooling — supplier capacity, design for manufacturability, line utilization and commercial terms — associated with a 3% reduction in CapEx spend. A further workstream standardized CapEx reporting across suppliers into one template covering spend, asset performance and amortization schedules, giving finance visibility into more than $100M of investments.
