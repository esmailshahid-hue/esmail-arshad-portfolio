## Challenge

I worked on sourcing for top-level assembly tooling in new product introduction. The equipment is bespoke, the qualified supplier list is short, and the programme timeline makes walking away expensive.

Comparing bespoke quotes against each other tells you which supplier is cheapest. It does not tell you whether the price is fair — and with no comparable prior purchase, there was no benchmark to assess a quote against at all.

## What I did

1. **Broke the tooling down into machining, material and labour inputs** instead of treating each quote as a single number.
2. **Costed every input independently**, so the resulting benchmark did not depend on any supplier's own figures.
3. **Assessed the quotes against that benchmark**, which surfaced charges the quoted totals did not show.
4. **Took the negotiation through the cost build-up line by line**, using each gap as a lever.

## Decision

> Instead of asking a supplier to justify a discount, I could question a specific cost input against an independent estimate of it. That changed what the negotiation was about.

## Outcome

**$150K in procurement savings** on the tooling package.
