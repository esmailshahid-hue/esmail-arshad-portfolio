## Context

Formlabs builds SLA and SLS 3D printing hardware. A single machine draws on hundreds of components across mechanical, optical and electronic categories, sourced from suppliers with lead times ranging from weeks to months.

The reporting covered more than 300 components across both platforms.

## Problem

On a bill of materials that size, one late component stops a build as effectively as a hundred. The information needed to see that coming — what is on hand, what is on order, what is late, what is committed — exists in the system, but split across inventory balances, open purchase orders and delivery exception records.

Nobody reads three hundred components across three data sources by hand every week. Shortages surface when a build cannot start.

## What I did

1. **Brought inventory coverage, open purchase orders and delivery exceptions into one query layer** in SQL and BigQuery, so component risk was assessed against all three at once rather than one at a time.
2. **Built shortage and exception reporting** across 300+ SLA/SLS components, running against current data rather than a periodic manual extract.
3. **Surfaced supply risk as an exception**, so planner attention went to the components that had moved rather than the full component list.
4. **Escalated to suppliers** on the components the reporting flagged, while there was still lead time available to recover.

## Result

**20% reduction in late orders**, with on-time in-full performance improving by 6%.

The reporting did not shorten a single supplier lead time. It moved the moment a shortage became visible earlier — far enough forward that expediting, re-planning or escalating was still possible, which is the whole difference between a supply risk and a late order.
