## Context

Formlabs builds SLA and SLS 3D printing hardware. A single machine draws on hundreds of components across mechanical, optical and electronic categories, with supplier lead times running from weeks to months. My reporting covered more than 300 of them.

## Problem

The data needed to see a shortage coming — inventory coverage, open purchase orders, delivery exceptions — sat in three separate places. At 300+ components across two platforms, that is not something anyone reviews by hand each week, so shortages tended to surface when a build could not start.

## What I did

- Pulled inventory coverage, open purchase orders and delivery exceptions into one query layer in SQL and BigQuery.
- Built shortage and exception reporting across the 300+ SLA/SLS components, running against current data rather than a periodic manual extract.
- Reported risk as an exception, so planner attention went to what had moved rather than the full component list.
- Escalated flagged components to suppliers while there was still lead time to recover.

## Result

**Late orders down 20%**, with on-time in-full performance up 6%.

The reporting did not change a single supplier lead time. It moved the point at which a shortage became visible far enough forward that expediting, re-planning or escalating was still an option.

## Related work

Two further workstreams ran in the same role. ABC analysis in Netstock across 1,200 SKUs segmented slow-moving, excess and at-risk inventory for disposition decisions, saving $50K in carrying costs. Separately, NetSuite spend and COGS reporting for SLS powders consolidated BOM costs and removed 80% of the manual reporting effort.
