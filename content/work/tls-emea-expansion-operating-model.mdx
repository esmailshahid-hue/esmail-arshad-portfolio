## Challenge

TLS was expanding ICT and data-centre hardware sales across EMEA, and every new market brought its own rules on importing, customs documentation, duties, product certifications and restricted goods.

A Bahrain order made the gap concrete. There was no reliable local importer-of-record structure behind it, and no process that would stop the next country from becoming the same problem again.

## What I did

1. **Led a ten-person cross-functional team**, splitting responsibilities across operations analysts, regional research analysts, sales and key account management by role and expertise.
2. **Owned the IOR onboarding workflow end to end** — vendor identification, RFQs, coverage, service assessment, due diligence, commercial negotiation, compliance validation and trial-shipment readiness.
3. **Established country-specific compliance protocols** covering shipping and customs documentation, physical-document requirements, duties and taxes, HS-code considerations, certifications and restricted-goods rules.
4. **Standardized the workflow across internal and external stakeholders**, defining responsibilities and SOPs for IOR partners, customs brokers, freight forwarders, operations analysts and account teams.
5. **Built performance controls around execution**, tracking fulfilment KPIs and customs-related delays, reviewed in weekly team meetings and monthly performance sessions.

## Decision

> Rather than treat each new country as a one-off customs problem, I built a repeatable market-entry process — IOR selection, country requirements, documentation, execution and performance control — that the next market could run through.

## Outcome

Over two quarters the team onboarded **10 IOR partners** and established a repeatable fulfilment and compliance model for EMEA.

That let TLS enter and service additional markets in the region, and **grew EMEA market share by 12%** against the company's position before the expansion effort.
